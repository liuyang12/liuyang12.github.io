Biography

Yang Liu is an assistant professor at the University of Hong Kong (HKU). He works on computational imaging, physical AI, and AI for science. He received a PhD degree in computer science from MIT, where he was fortunate to be advised by Prof. Frédo Durand in EECS & CSAIL. He spent a year as a postdoc scholar working on agentic AI for scientific imaging, hosted by Prof. Katie Bouman at Caltech CMS. He is excited about seeing, making, and connecting (almost) anything. He emphasizes agentic physical intelligence, structured diffusion image synthesis, and imaging and sensing beyond human vision (in terms of dimensionality and visibility) by combining optical imaging systems with compressive sensing and machine learning techniques.

Prior to this, he received bachelor’s and master’s degrees from Tsinghua University in 2016 and 2019, respectively. He worked on computational imaging and microscopy, advised by Prof. Qionghai Dai and working closely with Prof. Jinli Suo.

He is broadly interested in AI for meeting social needs and accelerating scientific discovery. He also worked on revealing the imaging threat of ambient light sensors on everyday screens, which is capable of resolving an image of the scene in front of the screen without accessing the selfie camera, and designing a privacy-preserving intradermal barcode and a robust recognition system as vaccine record and beyond (general on-patient medical record).
